Unit wt is the per-instrument position sign × min(vol target ÷ σ, cap) — what the sizing rule asks for on its own, marked ᶜ where the cap binds. Port wt divides that by the number of live markets, which is what you actually hold. Weights are sign × min(vol target ÷ σ, cap) ÷ N with σ the engine's EWMA vol (MOP Eq. 1). Ex-ante vol is √(w'Σw) using a correlation matrix over the last 750 trading days, shrunk 20% toward the average pairwise correlation; max possible vol is Σ|wᵢ|σᵢ, the ceiling if everything moved together. Risk shares sum to 100%. Strength is the trailing return divided by the standard deviation of that same k-month move, z = r · (σ√(k/12))⁻¹ — a raw percentage is the wrong unit, since a 2-year note down 1% at 1.2% vol has moved most of a standard deviation while natural gas down 1% at 42% vol has not moved at all. The ⚠ marks |z| < 0.5: the sign is close to a coin flip, so those positions are the likeliest to flip next month. The deadband, when on, is measured in the same units and takes those markets flat. Prices are the last close in the engine's cache, so fills are indicative. Signals and sizing output, not investment advice.
